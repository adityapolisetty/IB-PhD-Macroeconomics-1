The Solow model

What determines where capital settles? Saving, diminishing returns and the journey to a steady state.

Explore the model ↓Download notebook ↗

The intuition

Every year, an economy saves some of its output and loses some effective capital through depreciation and population growth. The difference determines whether capital per worker rises or falls.

The key force is diminishing returns. More capital produces more output, but each additional unit adds less than the previous one. Saving therefore rises more slowly than the amount needed to maintain a larger capital stock. Eventually the two flows balance.

A steady state is a balance of flows. Investment continues even when capital per worker stops changing.

The model

k˙=sAkα⏟saving−(δ+n)k⏟effective depreciation.\dot{k}=\underbrace{sAk^\alpha}_{\text{saving}}-\underbrace{(\delta+n)k}_{\text{effective depreciation}}.k˙=savingsAkα​​−effective depreciation(δ+n)k​​.

The positive steady state solves k˙=0\dot k=0k˙=0:

k∗=(sAδ+n) ⁣1/(1−α).k^*=\left(\frac{sA}{\delta+n}\right)^{\!1/(1-\alpha)}.k∗=(δ+nsA​)1/(1−α).

Below k∗k^*k∗, saving exceeds effective depreciation. Above k∗k^*k∗, the ranking reverses. Changing the initial condition changes the journey; changing the saving rate changes the destination.

Pause & predict

If the saving rate increases, does capital jump immediately to its new steady state?

Discuss the answer

No. The existing capital stock is predetermined. Higher saving immediately changes the rate of accumulation, and capital then moves gradually toward the higher steady state.

Experiment / Growth & convergence

At your starting point, capital rises. The steady state is 6.09; the initial change is 0.140 units per worker per year.

A = 1, α = ⅓, δ + n = 0.06. The exact solution is available for this calibration. Euler uses the current state to take each numerical step.

Take one numerical step

For a short interval Δt\Delta tΔt, Euler’s method turns a derivative into a change:

kt+Δt≈kt+Δt [sAktα−(δ+n)kt].k_{t+\Delta t}\approx k_t+\Delta t\,[sAk_t^\alpha-(\delta+n)k_t].kt+Δt​≈kt​+Δt[sAktα​−(δ+n)kt​]. 
change = s * A * k**alpha - (delta + n) * k
k_next = k + dt * change

The whole simulation repeats those two lines, using the updated state each time. In the experiment, reduce the time step and compare the Euler path with the exact path. Their agreement is a numerical check, separate from the economic claim that capital converges.

What to take away

Higher saving raises the steady-state level of output per worker in this model. Without technological growth, output per worker stops growing once the new steady state is reached. Faster growth during the transition is not a permanently higher growth rate.

Near the steady state, the derivative of the accumulation function is negative:

g′(k∗)=−(1−α)(δ+n).g'(k^*)=-(1-\alpha)(\delta+n).g′(k∗)=−(1−α)(δ+n).

This gives the speed of local convergence. Far from the steady state, the full nonlinear law of motion matters.

Pause & predict

At the steady state, is saving zero?

Discuss the answer

Saving is positive and exactly offsets effective depreciation. Zero net accumulation does not mean zero gross investment.
