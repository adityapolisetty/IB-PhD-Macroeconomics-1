## The intuition

A household can choose its initial consumption, but it cannot choose that number independently of its lifetime resources. A small initial mistake gets carried forward by the consumption growth rule. Over many years, that mistake changes the wealth path dramatically.

Choosing too much consumption eventually requires ever more borrowing. Choosing too little leaves resources unused. The optimal initial choice lies between these failures.

The subtle point is that **wealth itself need not approach zero**. The terminal condition concerns wealth measured in present-value units. A growing asset stock can have a shrinking present value when it grows more slowly than the interest rate.

## The model

Let $a$ denote assets and $c$ consumption. With a constant wage $w$ and interest rate $r$,

$$
\dot a=ra+w-c,\qquad \dot c=gc,\qquad g=\frac{r-\rho}{\sigma}.
$$

Here $\rho$ is the rate of time preference and $\sigma$ the inverse intertemporal elasticity of substitution. In the experiment, $r=0.05$ and $g=0.01$, so consumption grows at one percent per year.

Discounting the asset equation and integrating gives the lifetime budget relation:

$$
\int_0^\infty e^{-rt}c(t)\,dt=a_0+\frac{w}{r},
\qquad c(t)=c_0e^{gt}.
$$

For $r>g$, the optimal initial consumption is therefore

$$
c_0^*=(r-g)\left(a_0+\frac{w}{r}\right).
$$

The no-Ponzi restriction rules out an indefinitely growing discounted debt. With these preferences and an interior optimum, the household also leaves no valuable lifetime resources unused: $\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{-rt}a(t)=0$.

## Why a tiny mistake becomes visible

The asset path can be written as

$$
a(t)=-\frac{w}{r}+\frac{c_0}{r-g}e^{gt}
+\underbrace{\left(a_0+\frac{w}{r}-\frac{c_0}{r-g}\right)}_{\text{initial budget mismatch}}e^{rt}.
$$

At the optimal choice, the coefficient of $e^{rt}$ is zero. A guess below the optimum makes it positive; a guess above makes it negative. This fast-growing component is why an apparently small error in $c_0$ eventually dominates the simulation.

## The next code step

The numerical solver tracks both variables in a consistent order:

```python
def rhs(t, state):
    assets, consumption = state
    return [r * assets + wage - consumption,
            g * consumption]
```

An event function can stop the calculation when assets hit zero. That event is a useful diagnostic for an overly high guess in this example. Underconsumption instead needs a discounted-wealth check or a sufficiently long horizon.

## What to take away

Inspect the economically relevant quantity before judging a numerical path. An upward wealth curve is not enough to identify a terminal-condition failure. Discounting exposes the lifetime budget mismatch.
