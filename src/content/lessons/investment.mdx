## The intuition

A plant may want more capital without wanting enough to justify an adjustment cost. Waiting lets the benefit of adjustment grow until it is worth paying that cost. The resulting policy has an **inaction region**: a range of states where doing nothing is optimal.

A resale loss creates another reason to wait. A unit of capital costs one to buy but sells for less than one. A plant with excess capital may retain it rather than realise that loss immediately.

These frictions affect different margins. A fixed cost discourages small adjustments in either direction. A lower resale price directly makes selling less attractive.

## The model

Current profit is $\pi(k,z)=zk^\nu$, where $k$ is capital, $z$ productivity and $\nu\in(0,1)$ captures diminishing returns. Productivity is persistent and stochastic. The discount factor is $\beta$, and capital depreciates at rate $\delta$.

The plant compares two options:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_n(k,z)&=\pi(k,z)+\beta\mathbb E[V((1-\delta)k,z')\mid z],\\
V_a(k,z)&=\max_{k'}\{(1-f)\pi(k,z)-C(i)+\beta\mathbb E[V(k',z')\mid z]\},\\
V(k,z)&=\max\{V_n(k,z),V_a(k,z)\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Investment is $i=k'-(1-\delta)k$. Its transaction cost is

$$
C(i)=\begin{cases}i,&i\ge0,\\p_s i,&i<0.\end{cases}
$$

Here $p_s\le1$ is the resale price. Negative investment produces sale proceeds because $C(i)<0$. The fixed cost is a fraction $f$ of current profit, paid whenever the plant adjusts, independent of the adjustment's size.

  <p>No. Zero investment means k′ = (1 − δ)k. Capital falls through depreciation. Maintaining the capital stock requires replacement investment, which counts as adjustment in this model.</p>

## One comparison in code

At a given state, compute the best value with adjustment and the value without it:

```python
adjust = value_adjust > value_wait
next_capital = best_choice if adjust else (1 - delta) * capital
investment = next_capital - (1 - delta) * capital
```

The binary choice creates the inaction region. Conditional on adjustment, the plant still solves for the best size of the change. As a threshold is crossed, the chosen investment rate can jump.

## Reading the horizontal axis

Capital is divided by the user-cost benchmark

$$
k^b=\left(\frac{\nu z}{r+\delta}\right)^{1/(1-\nu)},\qquad r=\frac1\beta-1.
$$

This benchmark normalises the units. The full stochastic policy also depends on expected future productivity and the adjustment frictions; the benchmark alone does not determine it.

## What to take away

Doing nothing can be an active economic choice. The value of a small adjustment may not cover its cost. This generates waiting and occasional large changes, even while productivity and capital evolve continuously between decisions.
