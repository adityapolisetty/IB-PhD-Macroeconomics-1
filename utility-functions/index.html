The model connection

What these shapes do in economics

Keep the geometry close to the equations. These are the three links that make the pictures useful when you solve a model.

01 / marginal utility

A slope is a value at the margin

For an increasing utility function, u′(c) > 0. When it is concave, u″(c) < 0: the next unit still helps, but by less. The log and CRRA curves make that visible.

02 / optimal choice

A peak has zero slope

The quadratic example reaches its maximum where u′(c) = 0. A graph ending at the edge of the screen is not necessarily a mathematical maximum: inspect the domain and what happens beyond the plot.

03 / AK production

A constant slope changes growth

For f(k) = Ak, the marginal product stays at A as capital rises. With saving rate s, depreciation δ and population growth n, per-worker growth is sA − (δ + n).

For the utility tabs: If you only rank consumption choices, a strictly increasing transformation of utility keeps the same preferences. The displayed utility number, and even its numerical slope, can change. In intertemporal or expected-utility models, the specified flow utility and its curvature matter for choices across dates or states. The AK tab measures output, not utility.
